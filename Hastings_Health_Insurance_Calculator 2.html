How this estimate works (assumptions)
  • Net cost = annual employee premium (or minus the HSA deposit) + estimated out-of-pocket costs. Premiums are the “Caps Adjusted EE Contributions – Annual” amounts from the WMHIP 1/1/2027 illustration.
  • Negative amounts are HSA dollars. When the District’s cap exceeds the premium (a negative amount in the illustration), those dollars go into the employee’s HSA. They are shown as a negative premium and reduce the net cost. If the net total is negative, that is the HSA balance left over after your estimated costs. HSA dollars can only be used for qualified medical expenses.
  • The medical slider drives office visits, online visits, urgent care, ER and hospital / procedure charges. The Rx slider drives generic, preferred brand, non-preferred brand and preferred specialty fills. Preventive care is covered at 100% on every plan and is not counted.
  • Costs are worked through in order across the year: copays where a plan has them, deductible until it’s met, then coinsurance on the 80% plans (175/176 and Enhanced HSA 3500; you pay 20% up to the plan’s coinsurance maximum), all stopping at the out-of-pocket maximum. On every other plan the plan pays 100% of covered medical care after the deductible. Medical and Rx share one deductible and one out-of-pocket maximum.
  • Embedded deductible (Value HSA 6350): each person has their own deductible and out-of-pocket max, with a family limit. Aggregate deductible (all other plans): the whole family deductible must be met first. For Single coverage, individual amounts apply; for Two-Person and Full Family, family amounts apply. Value HSA 3000 also limits any one member to $6,350 within the family maximum.
  • Rx is subject to the deductible on every plan, so part of your Rx cost can be deductible dollars. When medical and Rx both count toward the same deductible, the split between the two follows the order care happens during the year.
  • Plans with “copay then 100% after deductible” (urgent care and ER on Enhanced 5000) are modeled as the copay plus the remaining charge going to the deductible. The ER copay is not waived for admissions, which is slightly conservative for Enhanced 5000.
  • Typical allowed charges used: primary care $150, specialist $250, online visit $75, urgent care $350, ER $2,000, generic Rx $25, preferred brand Rx $300, non-preferred brand Rx $450, preferred specialty Rx $3,000. Hospital / procedure charges rise with the medical slider.
  • Chiropractic, PT/OT/speech, private duty nursing, wisdom tooth extractions, massage therapy, non-preferred specialty drugs and out-of-network care are not modeled.
  • HSA tax savings are not included, and would lower the cost of the HSA plans further. The only HSA contribution counted is the District cap excess shown as a negative amount in the illustration.

This tool is an illustration only and is not a guarantee of actual costs. Actual costs depend on the care you use. Please refer to your plan summary and policy for full benefit details.
